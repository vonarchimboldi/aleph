What the concept does

A heuristic h estimates remaining goal cost. Greedy best-first orders by h; A* orders by g+h. Admissibility means h never overestimates true remaining cost. Consistency means h(n) ≤ cost(n,n′)+h(n′) on every edge, with h(goal)=0. Consistency prevents decreasing f-values along a path and supports graph A* without reopening closed states under standard nonnegative-cost assumptions; an admissible but inconsistent heuristic may require reopening.

A worked example

Use S→A cost 2, S→G cost 7, A→G cost 2. Let h(S)=4, h(A)=2, h(G)=0. A* first pops S, then A with f=4, then G with improved g=4. Greedy instead prefers the direct G because its h is zero, returning cost 7.

Independent problems

AI02-Q1

Trace greedy and A* on the worked graph, testing the goal on pop. Give the returned path cost for each.

Compare after attempting

Greedy: S,G, cost 7. A*: S,A,G, cost 4.

AI02-Q2

On the same graph, is h(S)=4, h(A)=1, h(G)=0 admissible? Is it consistent? Check each edge.

Compare after attempting

Admissible: true remaining costs are 4,2,0. Inconsistent on S→A because 4 > 2+1. Other edges satisfy consistency.

AI02-Q3

What does A* become when h is identically zero? Why is a heuristic with h(A)=10 invalid for the optimality guarantee here?

Compare after attempting

It becomes UCS. Since true remaining cost from A is 2, h(A)=10 overestimates and is not admissible.

Big picture and mind map

g: paid cost + h: estimated remainder → f → admissibility → consistency → reopening
