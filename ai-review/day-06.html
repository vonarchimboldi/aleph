What the concept does

To compute a posterior, first substitute observed evidence into the factors. Multiply factors containing a hidden variable, sum that variable out, and replace them with the resulting factor. Repeat until only query variables remain, then normalize. Summing out is not maximizing. Elimination order can change intermediate factor sizes and computational cost while leaving the exact answer unchanged.

A worked example

Let P(A=1)=0.2; P(B=1|A=1)=0.8 and P(B=1|A=0)=0.1. To find P(A=1|B=1), evidence restricts the B factor. The two unnormalized weights are 0.2×0.8=0.16 and 0.8×0.1=0.08. Normalize by their sum 0.24: posterior 2/3.

Independent problems

AI06-Q1

Compute P(B=1) and P(A=0|B=1) in the example. Show the sum and the normalization.

Compare after attempting

P(B=1)=0.16+0.08=0.24; P(A=0|B=1)=0.08/0.24=1/3.

AI06-Q2

For A→B→C with evidence C=c, write the unnormalized factor for query A after eliminating hidden B.

Compare after attempting

f(A)=P(A) Σ_b P(b|A)P(c|b). Normalize f(A) over A to obtain the posterior.

AI06-Q3

Factors f(X,Y), g(Y,Z), h(Z) are present, all variables binary. Eliminate Y first. Name the intermediate scope and its table size.

Compare after attempting

Multiply f and g to form scope {X,Y,Z} with 8 entries; sum Y out to produce a factor on {X,Z} with 4 entries. h(Z) need not be included until needed.

Big picture and mind map

query + evidence → restrict factors → multiply → sum hidden variables → normalize → compare orders
