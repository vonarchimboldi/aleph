The teaching cycle

  1. Find the small piece of knowledge the learner needs next.
  2. Explain it clearly and work through an example.
  3. Ask one focused question that uses it.
  4. Use the answer to decide whether to explain, practise or continue.
  5. Return to the idea on a later day and ask the learner to use it without notes.

What changes in our lessons

A lesson should contain several short explanation-and-practice cycles. Each idea gets a complete explanation before the next idea relies on it. A beginner needs both meaning and examples. Questions help us see what the learner can do after the teaching.

Keep first attempts, help and corrections separate. Start with similar problems while a method is new. Later, mix familiar methods so the learner must decide which one to use. A good result today does not by itself show what will be remembered next week.

Why his LLM tutoring account matters

When learning biology, Skycak reported that his tutor kept testing ideas it had not explained. He asked for narrow questions, actual instruction and smaller cycles. This is especially relevant to the problems with our earlier material.
