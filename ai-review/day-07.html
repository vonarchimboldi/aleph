Estimating a probability by drawing examples

Exact inference can require large tables. Sampling estimates probabilities from generated cases. We will see which cases each method uses, what it counts, and why a small estimate can differ from the exact answer.

1. An estimate is a proportion from generated cases

A sample is one generated case. If a binary variable is 1 in 6 of 10 samples, the sample estimate of its probability is 6/10=0.6.

The estimate can differ from the model’s exact probability because the generated cases are random. A second group of ten samples can produce a different estimate.

Forward sampling generates variables in an order where each variable’s parents are already sampled. Draw a root from its prior distribution. Then draw each child from the conditional row matching its sampled parents.

Follow the reasoning

Use A→B with P(A=1)=0.2, P(B=1|A=1)=0.8 and P(B=1|A=0)=0.1. First draw A. If A=1, draw B using probability 0.8. If A=0, draw B using probability 0.1.

Why would drawing B with probability 0.8 in every sample generate the wrong model?

Worked check · open after attempting

The probability 0.8 applies only when A=1. Using it when A=0 ignores the conditional table.

2. Rejection sampling keeps the cases matching the evidence

Suppose the evidence is B=1. Generate full cases and discard those with B=0. Among the accepted cases, count how often A=1.

If two accepted cases contain one A=1 and one A=0, the estimate is 1/2. We divide by accepted samples because the query is conditional on the evidence.

Rare evidence makes this method wasteful. If each generated case matches the evidence with probability 0.01, only about one in a hundred is accepted on average.

Follow the reasoning

Imagine generated cases (A,B)=(1,1),(0,0),(0,1),(0,0). Keep (1,1) and (0,1). One of the two kept cases has A=1, so the estimate is 0.5. The four generated cases are not the denominator of the conditional estimate.

AI07-Q1

Evidence occurs with probability 0.01. Out of 10,000 independent forward samples, what is the expected number accepted by rejection sampling? Is it guaranteed?

Worked check · open after attempting

Expected count 10000×0.01=100, not a guarantee; the count is random.

3. Likelihood weighting fixes the evidence instead

Likelihood weighting avoids rejecting a full case merely because its evidence value was different. Hold the evidence variables fixed. Sample the other variables, and give each sample a weight measuring how likely its fixed evidence is under its sampled parents.

For evidence B=1 in A→B, draw A from its prior. Set B=1 without drawing it. If A=1, assign weight P(B=1|A=1)=0.8. If A=0, assign weight 0.1.

The fixed evidence is not free evidence that every sample should count equally. The weights correct how much each generated case contributes.

4. Divide weighted support by total weight

To estimate P(A=1|B=1), add the weights of samples with A=1. Divide by the weights of all samples. This is a weighted proportion.

For the three samples in the table, the numerator is 0.8. The denominator is 0.8+0.1+0.1=1.0. The estimate is 0.8.

The exact answer for this model is 2/3, as shown in the previous lesson. A weighted estimate from only three random samples need not equal it. More suitable samples generally reduce sampling error, but finite estimates still vary.

Follow the reasoning

If a fourth sample has A=0 with weight 0.1, the estimate becomes 0.8/1.1=8/11. Notice that the numerator stays the same while the denominator grows.

5. Gibbs sampling updates one unobserved variable at a time

Gibbs sampling keeps a current assignment of all variables. Evidence variables stay fixed. Choose one non-evidence variable and draw a new value for it, conditional on the current values of the others.

In a Bayesian network, only certain neighboring variables are needed for this update: the variable’s parents, its children, and its children’s other parents. Together these are called its Markov blanket.

Why these neighbors? In the joint factorization, the variable appears in its own conditional factor and in the factors for its children. Factors not involving it cancel when we compare its possible values. Compute the remaining weights, normalize them, then draw a value.

Follow the reasoning

For A→B→C with B=1 fixed, an update of A uses weights proportional to P(A)P(B=1|A). Our two weights are 0.16 and 0.08, so A is redrawn with probability 2/3 for value 1. B stays fixed. C’s current value adds no factor involving A once B is given.

AI07-Q3

In A→B→C with B fixed as evidence, should a Gibbs update resample B? What determines an update to A?

Worked check · open after attempting

B remains fixed. The distribution for A is proportional to P(A)P(B|A), using its Markov blanket. Normalize before drawing.

6. Check what a sample count can and cannot show

Rejection sampling may discard most work when evidence is rare. Likelihood weighting can give many samples very small weights. Gibbs samples can be correlated with earlier samples and may take time to explore the distribution.

When checking an answer, state how samples were generated, which ones counted, and whether weights were used. Then write the numerator and denominator explicitly.

An expected accepted count is an average over repeated experiments. It is not a guarantee for one run. A finite sample estimate also does not become an exact answer merely because its arithmetic is correct.

Follow the reasoning

Compare an estimate with an exact answer on a small network before using sampling on a large one. A close match is a useful check. A mismatch may be ordinary sampling error or a mistake in how the cases were generated or counted.

AI07-W3

If a rejection sampler has no accepted samples, can you return zero as its estimate of the conditional query?

Worked check · open after attempting

No conditional estimate is available from that run: the denominator is zero. Generate more suitable samples or use another method. Zero accepted cases does not establish a zero conditional probability.

How the ideas connect

  • Generate cases from the stated model.
  • Rejection sampling counts only cases matching evidence.
  • Likelihood weighting fixes evidence and uses weighted proportions.
  • Gibbs keeps evidence fixed and redraws a non-evidence variable from its conditional distribution.
