What the concept does

Forward sampling draws each node given its already sampled parents. Rejection sampling discards samples inconsistent with evidence; rare evidence makes it wasteful. Likelihood weighting fixes evidence variables and multiplies each sample’s weight by their conditional evidence probabilities. Estimate the query using normalized weighted counts. Gibbs sampling updates a non-evidence variable conditioned on its Markov blanket. A finite estimate has sampling error; more samples do not turn a biased implementation into a correct one.

A worked example

In the binary A→B example from the previous lesson, suppose four forward samples contain exactly two with B=1 and only one of these has A=1. Rejection sampling estimates P(A=1|B=1) as 1/2 using the two accepted samples. It need not equal the exact 2/3 on a small sample. Likelihood weighting with evidence B=1 instead samples A from its prior and assigns weight 0.8 to A=1 and 0.1 to A=0.

Independent problems

AI07-Q1

Evidence occurs with probability 0.01. Out of 10,000 independent forward samples, what is the expected number accepted by rejection sampling? Is it guaranteed?

Compare after attempting

Expected count 10000×0.01=100, not a guarantee; the count is random.

AI07-Q2

Likelihood-weighted samples have A values 1,0,0 and corresponding weights 0.8,0.1,0.1. Estimate P(A=1|B=1).

Compare after attempting

Weighted query total 0.8 divided by total weight 1.0 gives 0.8. Finite-sample estimates vary from the exact posterior.

AI07-Q3

In A→B→C with B fixed as evidence, should a Gibbs update resample B? What determines an update to A?

Compare after attempting

B remains fixed. The distribution for A is proportional to P(A)P(B|A), using its Markov blanket. Normalize before drawing.
