What the concept does

A Bayesian network is a directed acyclic graph with one conditional probability table per node. Its joint distribution factors into the product of each node conditioned on its parents. Graph separation gives conditional independences that hold for every distribution factoring over the graph. A chain X→Y→Z and fork X←Y→Z are blocked by conditioning on the middle node. A collider X→Y←Z is blocked unless the collider or a descendant is observed. An active path means independence is not guaranteed; parameters may still create accidental independence.

A worked example

In Rain→Wet←Sprinkler with independent roots, Rain and Sprinkler are marginally independent. Conditioning on Wet activates the collider path. Observing wet ground can change how evidence for rain affects belief in sprinkler use: either cause may explain the observation.

Independent problems

AI05-Q1

Factor the joint for A→B→C. What conditional independence is guaranteed?

Compare after attempting

P(A,B,C)=P(A)P(B|A)P(C|B); A is independent of C conditional on B.

AI05-Q2

For A→C←B, compare conditioning on nothing, on C, and on a child D of C.

Compare after attempting

The collider path is blocked without evidence; observing C or its descendant D activates it. A and B are marginally independent in this graph but conditional independence is not guaranteed after those observations.

AI05-Q3

A is binary and B is binary with parent A. How many free scalar probabilities specify P(A) and P(B|A)?

Compare after attempting

One for the Bernoulli root A, and one for each of B’s two parent configurations: total 3.
