What the concept does

A proposition has a truth value; an interpretation assigns values to atomic propositions. P→Q is false exactly when P is true and Q false, and is equivalent to ¬P∨Q. Entailment means every model of the premises is a model of the conclusion. Test a proposed inference by looking for a model with true premises and false conclusion. In predicate logic, the domain, predicates and quantifier order matter: ∀x∃y allows a different witness for each x; ∃y∀x requires one witness working for all x.

A worked example

From P→Q and P, infer Q (modus ponens). From P→Q and Q, P does not follow: P=false,Q=true is a countermodel. Negating “every student solved some problem” gives “some student solved no problem”: ¬∀x∃y Solved(x,y) is ∃x∀y ¬Solved(x,y).

Independent problems

AI04-Q1

Does P→Q together with ¬Q entail ¬P? Prove it by eliminating the possibility P=true.

Compare after attempting

Yes. If P were true, the implication would force Q true, contradicting ¬Q. This is modus tollens.

AI04-Q2

Give a domain and a relation showing ∀x∃y R(x,y) does not entail ∃y∀x R(x,y).

Compare after attempting

Domain {1,2}; R(x,y) means x=y. Each x has its own witness, but neither single y equals every x.

AI04-Q3

Resolve the clauses (¬P∨Q) and (P∨R) on P. Does the resolvent alone imply both original clauses?

Compare after attempting

Resolvent Q∨R. No: P=true,Q=false,R=true satisfies Q∨R but falsifies ¬P∨Q.
