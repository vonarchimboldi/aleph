# Sources and what they support

Research date: November 14, 2026.

## Scope of the search

I surveyed Skycak’s chronological post list, curated blog, essay index, sitemap and book index. The merged index contains **1,175 article-link entries**, of which **389** match a broad study/teaching filter. That filter includes false positives, such as essays about learning careers and machine learning. It is an inventory aid, not a claim that 389 essays contain distinct study advice.

The 35 core sources below were read in full when short, or by relevant sections when long. The two book collections were surveyed through contents and relevant sections. Neither book was read cover to cover. The public PDF of *The Math Academy Way* contains 510 pages and identifies an August 13, 2026 update. The book page says its linked live document may be newer.

This is a broad public-source review. It cannot establish that every statement Skycak ever made, every untranscribed recording, or private/deleted material was found. The machine-readable inventory preserves this distinction.

Archive entry points: [chronological posts](https://www.justinmath.com/posts/), [curated blog](https://www.justinmath.com/blog/), [essays](https://www.justinmath.com/essays/), [books](https://www.justinmath.com/books/).

## Core sources

1. [](https://www.justinmath.com/how-to-learn-effectively-on-your-own/) — Work examples during instruction, practise each type independently, then review by solving problems. The suggested consecutive-success count and expanding intervals are rough guides.

2. [](https://www.justinmath.com/whats-the-best-way-to-teach-math-explicit-instruction-or-less-guided-learning/) — Skycak argues for explicit instruction for beginners. Use worked examples and practice, then reduce guidance as expertise increases. His strongest research generalizations remain his interpretation.

3. [](https://www.justinmath.com/the-math-academy-way-faq/) — Clarifies several distinctions: initial competence versus automaticity, grouped initial practice versus mixed review, and guided novice work versus expert exploration. This long FAQ was reviewed by relevant section.

4. [](https://www.justinmath.com/how-i-would-go-about-learning-a-subject-where-no-full-fledged-adaptive-learning-system-is-available/) — His biology-tutor account is the most direct source for this skill: narrow questions, actual new teaching, small teaching/practice cycles, one question at a time and saved learning history.

5. [](https://www.justinmath.com/q-and-a-4/) — Describes applying the approach outside mathematics. Treat the biology and music accounts as personal experience, not controlled evidence of a general learning-speed advantage.

6. [](https://www.justinmath.com/8-failure-modes-to-watch-out-for-in-math-learning-resources/) — A practical audit of teaching resources: missing intermediate steps, weak prerequisite checks, absent review, poor adaptation, unclear advancement criteria, insufficient successful practice, infrequent checks and weak follow-up.

7. [](https://www.justinmath.com/cognitive-science-of-learning-interleaving/) — Use mixed practice to make learners identify the right method. First establish enough competence to attempt the individual methods.

8. [](https://www.justinmath.com/cognitive-science-of-learning-spaced-repetition/) — Revisit material across time. Successful recall supports a longer interval. Failed recall calls for earlier repair and review. The article discusses research as well as scheduling choices.

9. [](https://www.justinmath.com/cognitive-science-of-learning-developing-automaticity/) — Practised component skills can reduce the attention needed for larger tasks. Initial competence and quick, reliable recall are different stages.

10. [](https://www.justinmath.com/cognitive-science-of-learning-minimizing-cognitive-load/) — Break large steps into smaller ones. Show worked examples, group related steps under meaningful goals, and use diagrams where useful. Gradually remove guidance.

11. [](https://www.justinmath.com/deliberate-practice-the-most-effective-form-of-active-learning/) — Choose manageable tasks that target the learner’s present weaknesses. Follow attempts with feedback and a changed next task.

12. [](https://www.justinmath.com/complete-individualization-an-often-forgotten-yet-critical-component-of-true-deliberate-practice/) — Learners who receive the same problem may need different next tasks after answering. Personalization must respond to demonstrated performance.

13. [](https://www.justinmath.com/who-needs-worked-examples-you-eventually/) — Being able to solve an early topic without examples does not imply that unsupported discovery will remain efficient as difficulty grows.

14. [](https://www.justinmath.com/the-importance-of-learning-your-prerequisites/) — Repair missing foundations that block more advanced work.

15. [](https://www.justinmath.com/how-math-academy-creates-its-knowledge-graph/) — Prerequisite connections and lesson sizes require subject expertise and repeated refinement. A generated graph is a proposal to inspect, not verified curriculum knowledge.

16. [](https://www.justinmath.com/the-most-important-thing-to-understand-about-building-educational-knowledge-graphs/) — Avoid introducing too many unfamiliar dependencies in one learning step. Split such steps into smaller lessons.

17. [](https://www.justinmath.com/overhelping-a-pathology-of-the-overinvolved-parent-or-tutor/) — Unrecorded help can hide what the learner can do and mislead an adaptive system. Record help honestly and verify independent performance later.

18. [](https://www.justinmath.com/the-vision-for-version-2-of-math-academys-spaced-repetition-system/) — The proposed design considers reference use and timing in addition to accuracy. Assisted completion should not receive the same scheduling credit as unaided recall.

19. [](https://www.justinmath.com/recall-before-rereading/) — For review, attempt recall before consulting the reference. This is not a reason to withhold explanations of new material.

20. [](https://www.justinmath.com/recall-first-reason-second/) — For already learned results, practise recall and then justification. Deriving a result and retrieving it automatically exercise different abilities.

21. [](https://www.justinmath.com/comfortable-fluency-in-consuming-information-is-not-a-proxy-for-learning/) — An explanation feeling easy to follow is weak evidence of being able to solve the problem independently.

22. [](https://www.justinmath.com/learning-is-about-bridge-building-not-jumping/) — Develop the component knowledge that makes a harder problem manageable. Do not use a long unproductive struggle as a substitute for teaching.

23. [](https://www.justinmath.com/active-learning-and-cognitive-load/) — Mental effort is not itself the goal. Use it for the learning task while removing unnecessary confusion and burden.

24. [](https://www.justinmath.com/study-sessions-should-be-short-and-frequent-as-opposed-to-long-and-sparse/) — Frequent shorter sessions can support habit and retention. The article also notes context-switching costs and practical schedule limits.

25. [](https://www.justinmath.com/spaced-repetition-is-more-than-memorization-its-also-generalization/) — Mix and space familiar skills so that learners must reconstruct context and choose methods, rather than repeat a cued procedure.

26. [](https://www.justinmath.com/deliberate-practice-has-a-strict-definition/) — Active work alone does not establish that practice is well designed. Attend to the specific target, feedback and adjustment.

27. [](https://www.justinmath.com/resolving-confusion-about-deliberate-practice/) — Distinguish targeted training from ordinary repetition and from performing a whole task without attention to weaknesses.

28. [](https://www.justinmath.com/the-counterintuitive-nature-of-effective-learning-strategies/) — Study methods that feel fluent can differ from methods that improve later performance. Check learning after a delay.

29. [](https://www.justinmath.com/the-pedagogically-optimal-way-to-learn-math/) — Use well-chosen small problems near the learner’s current ability. Save larger challenges for when their component skills are available.

30. [](https://www.justinmath.com/learning-math-is-like-climbing-a-ladder/) — Connect new learning to foundations the learner can actually use. Familiarity with a prerequisite’s name is insufficient.

31. [](https://www.justinmath.com/its-easy-to-think-you-know-the-prerequisites-when-in-fact-you-dont/) — Check prerequisite knowledge with actual tasks rather than relying on confidence.

32. [](https://www.justinmath.com/one-of-the-most-comforting-things-a-struggling-math-learner-can-hear/) — Explain that missing prior knowledge can make a topic feel difficult and can be repaired. Avoid treating difficulty as a verdict on ability.

33. [](https://www.justinmath.com/frequent-low-stakes-quizzes-are-such-a-powerful-learning-tool/) — Use short checks to practise recall and locate gaps before they accumulate.

34. [](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/) — Describes a specialized model for review in prerequisite graphs. Do not claim that a simple tutor prompt implements this system or estimates its weights.

35. [](https://www.justinmath.com/spaced-repetition-vs-spiraling/) — Keep later review responsive to the learner rather than assuming that a fixed return to each topic is equivalent to individualized spacing.

## Longer collections

- [The Math Academy Way](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf): a working draft credited to Justin Skycak, with Jason Roberts as adviser. Useful for locating discussions of memory, explicit instruction, practice, prerequisite graphs and review. The live document is linked from the author’s book page.
- [Advice on Upskilling](https://www.justinmath.com/files/advice-on-upskilling.pdf): a collection of shorter pieces. Relevant sections discuss practice, recall, prerequisites, consistency and study routines. Much of it overlaps with individual posts. It is not independent evidence in addition to those posts.

## Our adaptations

The skill’s GATE DA examples, HTML design, attempt-record fields, mentor verification and subject plans are local design choices. They should not be attributed to Skycak. So are the choices to use calm language, respect existing study capacity, and avoid automatic mastery claims from a small number of correct answers.

A tutor prompt can guide decisions. It does not itself supply a verified question bank, a persistent learner model, automatic scheduling or Math Academy’s software.

## Research claims

The essays cite research but also contain interpretation, personal experience and product arguments. When a claim about learning science matters to a decision, consult the original study or review. The operational recommendations here do not require adopting all of Skycak’s stronger claims about discovery, general problem-solving ability or educational acceleration.
