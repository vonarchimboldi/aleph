Graphs: reachability, discovery order and counterexamples

DSA Round 3 · Day 7 · December 1 · 90-minute session

Begin without reopening the earlier unit or its worked answers. Record whether you used notes, hints or a remembered solution. A correct reconstruction after exposure is useful practice, but an independent transfer claim needs a fresh problem selected by the mentor.

Budget: 30 minutes for the two checks; 25 for diagnosing and repairing the first broken step; 20 for a fresh mentor-selected problem; 15 for synthesis and a revised plan. Return to the earlier exposition only after preserving your first attempt.

R2-DSA-07-T-Q1 · delayed

A known shortest unweighted path is A–B–C–D, of length 3. Could A–C be an additional edge? Could A–D? Justify using shorter paths.

Worked check · open after attempting

Neither: A–C–D would have length 2 and A–D length 1, contradicting the stated shortest distance.

R2-DSA-07-T-Q2 · transfer

A DAG has edges P→Q,R→Q,Q→S,Q→T. Test P,R,Q,T,S and P,Q,R,S,T as topological orders. Give the violating edge for any failure.

Worked check · open after attempting

First is valid. Second violates R→Q because R appears after Q. This is an adjacent structural extension of the basic graph syllabus; give it lower priority than traversal and shortest paths.

Big picture

Traversal order, reachable set and shortest-path distance answer different questions. Universal claims need proofs or small counterexamples.

Identify whether the question asks for order, a set, or distance. → Write the specified neighbor order before tracing. → Reverse an entire path to reason about a reversed graph. → Disprove a universal claim with the smallest legal graph.

Use the error to choose the next task

  • Assuming BFS and DFS have the same visitation order
  • Using numeric vertex order instead of adjacency order
  • Checking a necessary constraint as though it were sufficient
