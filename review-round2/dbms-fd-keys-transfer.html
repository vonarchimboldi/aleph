R2-DBMS-04-T-Q1 · delayed

R(A,B,C), F={A→B,B→C}, decomposed into AB and AC. Is it lossless? Does it preserve B→C? Give a legal component-instance counterexample for preservation.

Worked check · open after attempting

Lossless because intersection A determines AB. Not dependency-preserving: AB={(a1,b),(a2,b)}, AC={(a1,c1),(a2,c2)} satisfy projected A→B,A→C but their join violates B→C when c1≠c2.

R2-DBMS-04-T-Q2 · transfer

R(A,B,C,D), F={A→BC,C→D,B→A}. Find candidate keys and test BCNF.

Worked check · open after attempting

A and B are candidate keys. C+={C,D}, so nontrivial C→D has a determinant that is not a superkey; BCNF fails.

Big picture

A closure is a fixed-point computation. A candidate key also needs minimality, and a decomposition has separate information and constraint properties.

Compute closure until no rule adds an attribute. → Find attributes absent from every RHS. → Enumerate minimal determinants rather than stopping after the first key. → Check BCNF for every nontrivial FD; test preservation separately from losslessness.

Use the error to choose the next task

  • Applying AB→C when only A is known
  • Calling a superkey a minimal key
  • Concluding lossless implies dependency-preserving
