Opening check · before reading

Is the definite integral of a negative function a negative geometric area?

Start with a thin strip

Imagine slicing a region vertically. At horizontal position x, the strip extends from a lower graph to an upper graph. Its height is upper value minus lower value; its small area is that height times its width. Adding those strips gives an integral.

A=∫ab(fupper(x)−flower(x)) dx.A=\int_a^b\bigl(f_{\mathrm{upper}}(x)-f_{\mathrm{lower}}(x)\bigr)\,dx.

The subtraction order is geometry, not a rule determined by the names f and g. First solve their intersection equation. Between consecutive intersections, test a point to find which graph is higher. If the order changes, split the integral. Equivalently, area is the integral of the absolute difference, but that notation does not remove the need to understand its sign.

A signed integral can cancel contributions from different regions. Geometric area cannot cancel. For instance, the integral of x from −1 to 1 is zero, but the region between its graph and the x-axis has area 1. The left triangle and the right triangle each contribute a positive half.

Sometimes horizontal slices are simpler. Then integrate right boundary minus left boundary with respect to y. Choose the slicing direction that describes the whole region with the fewest changes. Check that the intersections really enclose a bounded region; intersecting curves alone need not determine the region intended by a question.

Two worked examples · follow the decisions

Example 1 · One region, no change in ordering

Find the area between y=x and y=x².

Solve x=x²: x=0 or 1. On (0,1), x>x²; at x=1/2 the values are 1/2 and 1/4. Thus A=∫01(x−x2)dx=[x2/2−x3/3]01=1/6A=\int_0^1(x-x^2)dx=[x^2/2-x^3/3]_0^1=1/6. This positive result matches the picture of a narrow region inside the unit square.

Example 2 · Two pieces that cancel if treated as signed

Find the total bounded area between y=x and y=x³.

Intersections satisfy x³=x, so they occur at −1, 0, 1. On (−1,0), x³ is above x; on (0,1), x is above x³. The areas are equal by symmetry. Therefore A=2∫01(x−x3)dx=2(1/2−1/4)=1/2A=2\int_0^1(x-x^3)dx=2(1/2-1/4)=1/2. Integrating x−x³ straight from −1 to 1 would incorrectly give zero for the total area.

Problems 1–3 · Concept and mechanics

C03-P1

Find the area between y=2x and y=x².

Worked solution — open after an attempt

Intersections are 0 and 2, with 2x above x² between them. Integrate 2x−x22x-x^2 from 0 to 2 to obtain 4/3.

C03-P2

Find the area between y=4−x² and the x-axis.

Worked solution — open after an attempt

The bounded region runs from −2 to 2 and lies above the axis. Its area is [4x−x3/3]−22=32/3[4x-x^3/3]_{-2}^2=32/3.

C03-P3

Find the area between y=x and the x-axis on [−2,1].

Worked solution — open after an attempt

Split at zero: ∫−20(−x)dx+∫01xdx=2+1/2=5/2\int_{-2}^0(-x)dx+\int_0^1x dx=2+1/2=5/2.

Problems 4–5 · Application and explanation

C03-P4

Find the total bounded area between y=2x and y=x³. Identify the upper graph in each component.

Worked solution — open after an attempt

Intersections solve x(x²−2)=0, giving −√2, 0, √2. On (−√2,0), x³ is above 2x; on (0,√2), 2x is above x³. Symmetry gives twice the integral of 2x−x³ from 0 to √2: 2[ x²−x⁴/4 ]₀^√2=2.

C03-P5

Find the area bounded by x=y² and x=2−y.

Worked solution — open after an attempt

Intersections satisfy y²+y−2=0, so y=−2 or 1. The right curve is 2−y on this interval. Integrate 2−y−y22-y-y^2 from −2 to 1 to get 9/2. Horizontal slices avoid splitting a square-root graph into branches.

Exit check · close examples and solutions

Find the area enclosed by y=3x and y=x², labeling intersections and upper curve.

Worked solution — open after an attempt

Intersections 0 and 3; upper curve 3x. Area ∫03(3x−x2)dx=9/2\int_0^3(3x-x^2)dx=9/2.

If something broke

If a result is negative, revisit slice height before taking an absolute value of the final answer. If intersections are missing, solve the full factored equation. Fresh repair: y=x and y=2x² enclose area 1/24.

The big picture · reconstruct the map

Text version of the map
  • Describe the region: Find all intersections; Check it is bounded.
  • Choose slices: Vertical: upper minus lower; Horizontal: right minus left.
  • Respect signs: Split at ordering changes; Geometric area cannot cancel.
  • Accumulate: Integrate nonnegative slice length; Use symmetry only when justified.
  • Check the result: Units squared; positive magnitude; Compare with a rough rectangle.
