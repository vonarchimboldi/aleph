Calculus · Thirty study days

Understand, practise, then repair.

A three-phase calculus review. Phase 1 is ready below; Phases 2 and 3 are the next stages of the plan.

Days are relative study days. No calendar start or submission deadline has been assigned.

1 · Refresher

Days 1–10. Exposition, worked examples, five practice problems, a fresh exit check, and a big-picture mind map each day.

2 · Standard problem types

Days 11–20. Build a problem-type library across ISI MSQE, IIT JAM, CMI, DSE MA Economics and GATE DA. Match the relevant exam scope and distinguish genuine past questions from original companion problems. Material not yet authored.

3 · Review and remedy

Days 21–30. Use Phase 2 submissions to select targeted repairs, worked corrections, fresh transfer checks and delayed reattempts. The exact daily mix will depend on the errors observed. Material not yet authored.

Start with the meaning, then learn the move

Work through an explanation before attempting practice. Follow worked examples with a pencil: name the problem, choose the method, check its conditions, carry out the calculation, and interpret the result. Then close the example and solve a nearby problem independently.

The limits and integrals lessons contain broader reference banks, not just two examples. IVT has twelve original applications; MVT has twelve companion examples linked to the twelve lessons in your selected playlist. Use a subset in the first sitting and return to the rest. Covering every example and video can take longer than one sitting.

Begin Day 1: limits

Phase 1 · The ten lessons

  1. Day 1 · Computing limits

    Choose a method from the expression, and distinguish a limit from a value.

  2. Day 2 · Computing integrals

    Recognize substitution, integration by parts, and partial fractions, then check by differentiation.

  3. Day 3 · Areas between curves

    Translate a drawn region into nonnegative slice lengths and split where the ordering changes.

  4. Day 4 · Applications of the Intermediate Value Theorem

    Use continuity to prove existence, then supply a separate argument if uniqueness is needed.

  5. Day 5 · Applications of the Mean Value Theorem

    Turn derivative information into bounds on total change; check endpoints and differentiability.

  6. Day 6 · Convexity and concavity

    Connect chord geometry, derivative monotonicity, curvature, and optimization without reversing signs.

  7. Day 7 · Convergence of series

    Separate terms from partial sums and select a convergence test with valid hypotheses.

  8. Day 8 · Power series and Taylor expansions

    Find a convergence interval including endpoints, and separate finite Taylor approximation from infinite-series equality.

  9. Day 9 · Monotone convergence and Bolzano–Weierstrass: foundations

    Distinguish a convergent sequence from a convergent subsequence and prove monotone convergence using a least upper bound.

  10. Day 10 · Monotone convergence and Bolzano–Weierstrass: applications

    Use sequence theorems in recurrences, partial sums, bisection, and a cumulative refresher check.

Days 9 and 10 deliberately use the repeated topic from the requested list: first the foundations, then applications and consolidation. Monotone convergence here concerns real sequences.

The course’s big picture

Theorems are bridges between these descriptions. IVT gives existence from continuity; MVT turns slope into total change; FTC connects accumulation and differentiation; sequence theorems control limiting processes.
