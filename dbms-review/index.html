Seven days of material

  1. Day1 · Relations, ER models, keys, constraints and types

    Turn a business rule into keys and participation constraints, then test updates against them.

  2. Day2 · Relational algebra and tuple calculus

    Translate set and quantifier statements into a reproducible query result.

  3. Day3 · SQL: joins, groups, NULL and correlated queries

    Compute query outputs from the actual rows while tracking duplicate and null behavior.

  4. Day4 · Functional dependencies, keys and normalization

    Derive keys and assess normal forms from dependencies, then test what a decomposition preserves.

  5. Day5 · Files, index choice and B+ tree calculations

    Translate a page layout into an inequality and choose an index from the query predicate.

  6. Day6 · Warehouses, OLAP, hierarchies and measures

    Define the fact grain, distinguish cuboids from result rows, and aggregate measures correctly.

  7. Day7 · Preprocessing: normalization, discretization, sampling and compression

    Choose a transformation from its goal, preserve its assumptions, and distinguish numerical scaling from schema normalization.

Every day has two multi-select questions and three short computations/explanations, ten learning-diagnostic points. The examples are original; the linked official papers establish problem families, not copied prompts or answers. Questions are designed to expose the first mistaken assumption, not just a wrong final number.

The big picture

A logical schema defines permitted information. A query defines a result under specific semantics. Storage determines access cost. Analytical summaries and transformations must preserve the information needed for their purpose.
